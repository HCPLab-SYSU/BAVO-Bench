Recovering the View: Benchmarking Physical Active Vision for Occlusion Recovery in Robotic Manipulation

Kaijun Luo1, Yudi Huang2, Qijun Zhong1, Xinshuai Song1, Yang Liu1,4†, Liang Lin1,3,4
1 Sun Yat-sen University 2 University of Electronic Science and Technology of China 3 Pengcheng Laboratory 4 X-Era AI Lab

† Corresponding author

Paper Github

Abstract

Physical active vision allows robots to change their viewpoint when task-relevant observations become unreliable. However, existing manipulation benchmarks provide limited support for studying how policies recover from occlusion during execution. We introduce BAVO-Bench, a bimanual active-vision benchmark that systematically controls external visibility through Clean, Stage Occlusion, and Random-time Occlusion conditions. We further introduce Camera-attributable Visibility Gain (CVG), a counterfactual metric that isolates visibility improvements attributable to camera motion rather than scene dynamics. Building on this setting, we present A-FAR, a policy for joint viewpoint and manipulation control. A-FAR combines robot-centric 3D observations with spatial context encoding to model geometric relationships consistently across viewpoints. It further leverages future 3D motion supervision from a pretrained 4D model to capture how task-relevant scene geometry evolves during manipulation. Experiments in simulation and the real world demonstrate improved robustness to structured and temporally unexpected occlusions, while maintaining strong performance under clean observations. Further analyses reveal that recovering visibility alone does not guarantee task success, highlighting the importance of translating recovered visual information into effective manipulation.
